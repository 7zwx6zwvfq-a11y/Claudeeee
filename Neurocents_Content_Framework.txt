━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
MASTER FORMULA (applies to EVERYTHING)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Every unit of content — title, thumbnail, hook, 30-second block, full video — follows:

    QUESTION / CONFLICT → RESOLUTION → NEW QUESTION / CONFLICT → RESOLUTION

This loop never ends. The moment there's no open question, the viewer leaves.
Apply at every level: thumbnail text, hook line, every 30 seconds of script, end of video.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PRINCIPLE 1 — THE BRAIN DECIDES BEFORE YOU
(Click is emotional, not rational)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The viewer does not consciously decide to watch. They feel an urgent need to know
something — and click before thinking. This has two consequences:

→ TITLE + THUMBNAIL must create an imperative need, not describe a topic.
  Bad: "Understanding the Sunk Cost Fallacy"
  Good: "Why Losing Money Makes You Want to Lose More"

→ THE FIRST SECOND OF THE VIDEO must confirm the promise.
  A large % of viewers clicked on pure impulse. If the first second doesn't
  deliver what they came for, they leave immediately.
  Rule: In the first 5 seconds, Alex must reference EXACTLY what the title/thumbnail promised.
  If title = "Why you want it LESS after buying" → open with wanting/buying/disappointment immediately.
  Never open with context, backstory, or introductions.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PRINCIPLE 2 — THE CURIOSITY TRAP
(Gaps in everything, at every level)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Every element must generate demand through unresolved curiosity:

TITLE:
- Plant a question the brain MUST resolve
- Never answer in the title
- Formula: counterintuitive statement + unresolved implication

THUMBNAIL TEXT:
- One short phrase that opens a loop (max 5 words)
- Must create a question, not state a fact

HOOK (first 10 seconds):
- Line 1: Pattern interrupt (surprising fact or scene)
- Line 2: Personal consequence for the viewer
- Line 3: Open loop that forces them to keep watching
- Never open with "In this video..." or a direct question (too weak)
- Always open with a FACT or a SCENE

SCRIPT — EVERY 30 SECONDS:
- Every block ends with an unresolved question or upcoming reveal
- Phrases: "But that's not the part that costs you money..."
            "The real reason is something nobody talks about..."
            "And what they found next changed everything..."
- Alex and brain villain carry the conflict — use their dynamic as the tension mechanism

CURIOSITY GAP TYPES (rotate these):
  → Belief breaking: "Everything you know about X is backwards"
  → Hidden mechanism: "The real reason X happens is Y"
  → Surprising consequence: "X doesn't just affect Y — it affects Z"
  → Identity challenge: "Smart people do this more, not less"


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PRINCIPLE 3 — BEING ORIGINAL IS OVERRATED
(Validated topics + 3-6% innovation)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Video #25 of a proven topic beats video #1 of an original topic.
People click because the TOPIC interests them, not because the video is unique.

TOPIC SELECTION PROCESS:
1. Search the topic on YouTube — does it have videos with 100K+ views?
2. Check what top behavioral finance / psychology of money channels are doing
3. Identify the proven angle that generates views
4. Add 3-6% differentiation: better hook, stronger animation, deeper science
5. Never create a topic because it's interesting to you — only if there's proven demand

COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS (do this before every video):
- Search the topic → sort by view count → study the top 3 thumbnails + titles
- What question are they opening? What gap are they creating?
- Take that structure and make it Neurocents: Alex + brain villain + behavioral finance angle

THE RULE: Don't innovate the topic. Innovate the execution.


━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
PRINCIPLE 4 — GUIDE THE VIEWER
(Viewer journey: discovery → binge → subscribe → loyal → client)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
People WANT to be guided. They want you to tell them what to watch next.
If you don't guide them, they watch one video and leave.

TARGET JOURNEY:
  New viewer → watches 3-5 videos → subscribes → loyal viewer → potential client

TOOLS TO BUILD THE JOURNEY:

[CTA AT 33% — minute 2-3 of every video]
Alex looks at camera. Brain villain reacts.
Script: "If your brain is doing this to you right now — subscribe.
         We break down a new bias every week. It's free, and it might
         save you more than you think."
Duration: max 5 seconds. Never interrupt momentum.

[NAME DROP — inside the video]
At the 50-60% mark, plant the seed for the next video:
"We'll cover exactly why [related bias] works the same way — in the next video."
This creates demand for a video that doesn't exist yet.

[END SCREEN — last 20 seconds]
Alex says: "Watch [exact title] next — it explains why [curiosity gap]."
End screen card appears simultaneously.
Never just say "watch more videos" — name the specific next video.

[PINNED COMMENT — post immediately after publishing]
First comment = yours.
Content:
  Line 1: The main insight restated as a question
  Line 2: Link to the most relevant related video
  Example: "Would you still hold if you bought today? Watch this next: [link]"

[DESCRIPTION STRUCTURE]
  Line 1: Curiosity gap restated as a question (makes people click "more")
  Line 2: What they'll understand after watching
  Line 3: Link to playlist or next video
  Tags: behavioral finance, psychology, investing, money mindset, neuroscience, brain biases

PRINCIPLE 5 — STORYTELLING IS EVERYTHING
(Cohesive narrative across the entire channel)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Storytelling is what converts viewers into loyal subscribers.
For Neurocents, every element communicates ONE cohesive story:

THE CHANNEL STORY:
  "Your brain has been running programs that cost you money your entire life.
   Alex discovers them one by one — and so do you."
  Alex = the viewer's avatar. Brain villain = the enemy they recognize in themselves.

VISUAL LANGUAGE (must be 100% consistent):
- Color palette: always the same across thumbnails, video backgrounds, text
- Typography: same font family, same weight, same style in every thumbnail
- Animation style: same character proportions, same line thickness, same expression range
- Brain villain: consistent design, consistent behavior across all videos
  Every element communicates the same story — colors, font, animation all say "Neurocents."

WITHIN-VIDEO STORY ARC (every video):
  Act 1: Alex encounters the bias in real life (relatable scene)
  Act 2: Brain villain is revealed as the mechanism (science explanation)
  Act 3: The cost ($ or life consequence) becomes clear
  Act 4: Alex gains awareness — and so does the viewer

BETWEEN-VIDEO STORY ARC (across the channel):
  Each video = one episode in Alex's journey of discovering his biases
  Reference previous episodes: "Remember when Alex bought that stock..."
  Build continuity — loyal viewers feel like they're following a character

STORYTELLING RULE:
  Don't just explain a concept. Show Alex experiencing it.
  The viewer must see themselves in Alex before they can learn from him.

TITLE FORMULA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Rules:
- Validated topic first (proven demand)
- Curiosity gap: question the brain MUST resolve
- Emotional trigger (click is always emotional)
- Short, counterintuitive, open loop
- Never answer in the title

Formulas:
  → "Why [surprising thing] [counterintuitive outcome]"
  → "The [hidden mechanism] That [unexpected consequence]"
  → "Your Brain [does X] — And It's [costing/breaking/ruining] [thing viewer cares about]"

Examples:
  ✅ "Why Losing Money Makes You Want to Lose More"
  ✅ "Why Do You Want It LESS the Second You Buy It?"
  ✅ "The Billion-Dollar Bet Your Bank Wins Every Single Day"
  ❌ "How to Avoid the Sunk Cost Fallacy" (answer is in the title, no gap)

THUMBNAIL FORMULA (MrBeast Rule)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
3 elements only:
  1. ALEX looking directly at camera (fear, shock, or realization — never neutral)
  2. ONE object that represents the concept
  3. ONE short text = the open loop (max 5 words)

Rules:
- 100% saturated background (orange, red, yellow — never white or grey)
- Readable at 20 feet
- Text CREATES the gap, does not answer it
- Brain villain optional — adds narrative tension when included
- Thumbnail text + title must create a combined gap (not repeat the same thing)

TikTok cover (9:16):
- Same 3 elements, vertical composition
- Text in top 25% of frame
- Bottom 15% empty (TikTok UI safe zone)
- 3-second native hook recorded to camera before every repurposed clip

SCRIPT STRUCTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[0–5s] PROMISE FULFILLMENT
Immediately confirm what title/thumbnail promised. No context. No intro. No "welcome back."
If they clicked for X, give them X in the first sentence.

[5–15s] HOOK
- Surprising fact or scene (pattern interrupt)
- Personal consequence for viewer
- Open loop that demands continuation
Science = authority for faceless channel: name researcher + year + institution

[15s – min 2] ACT 1: ALEX EXPERIENCES THE BIAS
Show the relatable scene. Viewer sees themselves in Alex.
End with an unresolved question.

[min 2–3] CTA (33% mark)
Alex at camera. Brain villain reacts. 5 seconds max.
"Subscribe — new bias every week."

[min 3 – 60%] ACT 2: BRAIN VILLAIN REVEALED + SCIENCE
Explain the mechanism through Alex vs brain villain dynamic.
Curiosity gap every 30 seconds without exception.
Mid-video name drop: "In the next video, we cover [X]..."

[60–80%] ACT 3: THE COST
Make the consequence concrete: specific $ amount, specific life decision.
This is where the viewer realizes the stakes.

[80–100%] ACT 4: AWARENESS + GUIDE
Alex gains awareness. Viewer gains awareness with him.
Name the next video explicitly.
End screen card appears.
Final line = seed for next curiosity gap.
