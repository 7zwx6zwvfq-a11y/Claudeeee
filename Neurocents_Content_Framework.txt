1. TITLE FORMULA
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Rules:
- Validated topic first (proven demand in the niche — check what's already working)
- Curiosity gap: plant a question the brain MUST resolve
- Emotional trigger, not rational (click is always emotional)
- Short, counterintuitive, open loop
- Never explain the answer in the title

Formula options:
  → "Why [surprising thing] [counterintuitive outcome]"
  → "The [hidden mechanism] That [unexpected consequence]"
  → "Your Brain [does X] — And It's [costing/breaking/ruining] [thing viewer cares about]"

Examples:
  ✅ "Why Losing Money Makes You Want to Lose More"
  ✅ "Why Do You Want It LESS the Second You Buy It?"
  ✅ "The Billion-Dollar Bet Your Bank Wins Every Single Day"
  ❌ "How to Avoid the Sunk Cost Fallacy" (answer is in the title, no gap)

2. THUMBNAIL FORMULA (MrBeast Rule)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
3 elements only:
  1. ALEX looking directly at camera (face = emotional connection)
  2. ONE object that represents the concept (money, brain, chart, etc.)
  3. ONE short text = the open loop (max 5 words, 100% saturated color)

Rules:
- 100% saturated background color (orange, red, yellow — never white or grey)
- Readable at 20 feet (phone thumbnail size)
- Text must CREATE the gap, not answer it
- Brain villain optional as 2nd character — adds narrative tension
- Alex's expression = fear, shock, or realization (never neutral)

For TikTok cover (9:16):
- Same 3 elements, vertical composition
- Text in top 25% of frame
- Bottom 15% empty (TikTok UI safe zone)
- Re-record 3-second native hook before the clip

3. SCRIPT STRUCTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

[SECONDS 0–15] HOOK + AUTHORITY + PROMISE FULFILLMENT
- Open with a REAL scientific study, experiment, or data point
  → Name: researcher, year, institution
  → Example: "In 1954, neuroscientists James Olds and Peter Milner at McGill University..."
- The science IS the credential (faceless channel = science as authority)
- Immediately confirm what the title/thumbnail promised
  → Viewer clicked for a reason — give them the payoff signal in the first 10 seconds
  → If title = "Why you want it less", the hook must reference wanting/buying/disappointment immediately
- End the hook with a NEW open loop that keeps them watching

[EVERY 30 SECONDS] MICRO CURIOSITY GAPS
- Every block must end with an unresolved question or an upcoming reveal
- Formula: "But here's what nobody tells you..." / "And that's only half the problem..." / "The real reason is [pause] coming up"
- Alex and brain villain are the story vehicle — use their dynamic to carry the conflict

[MINUTE 2–3 = 33% MARK] CTA
- Natural, not forced
- Example: "If this is making sense, subscribe — we go deeper every week."
- Alex looks at camera, brain villain reacts

[MIDDLE] STORY ARC
- Alex = the viewer (experiencing the bias)
- Brain villain = the mechanism (personifies the psychological force)
- Real-world example → science explanation → consequence → recognition
- Every concept needs: a human story + the research + the $ cost

[LAST 60 SECONDS] GUIDE THE VIEWER
- Explicitly name the next video: "Next week we're covering [X] — watch it before you [decision]"
- End screen: next video card must appear here
- Final line plants a seed for the next curiosity gap

4. HOOK FORMULA (First 10 seconds)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Structure:
  LINE 1: Surprising fact or counterintuitive statement (the pattern interrupt)
  LINE 2: The consequence for the viewer personally
  LINE 3: The open loop that keeps them watching

Example (N4 — dopamine):
  "In 1954, two scientists found the override switch for every brain on the planet."  ← pattern interrupt
  "The rats pressed it until they died."                                              ← consequence
  "You pressed it this morning."                                                      ← open loop

Rules:
- Never start with "In this video I'm going to..."
- Never start with a question (too weak)
- Always open with a FACT or a SCENE, not a statement about the video
- The viewer should feel the hook is about THEM, not about a topic

  5. Max 1-2 clips/day, minimum 4 hours apart
  6. Best days: Tuesday + Thursday | Best times: 9-11am, 10am-12pm

TikTok description formula:
  LINE 1: Hook from the clip (the moment that creates the question)
  LINE 2: The unresolved gap (what they don't know yet)
  LINE 3: CTA → YouTube → Neurocents
